A project briefing answers *what is going on in this project?* — for a PM joining mid-stream, for a colleague covering while you are away, or for you after two months on something else.

Where [deriving](/derive/derived-documents) works from one source document, a briefing works across the whole project.

## Generate a briefing

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the briefing dialog">
    Choose **Generate project briefing**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a model">
    A briefing reads widely across the project, so it is one of the places where a stronger model earns its cost. See [AI credit and models](/ai/credit-and-models).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate">
    Choose **Generate**. The briefing runs as a background task — you can keep working and pick up the output from the **Agents** section when it completes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What a briefing contains

| Section | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| **Product snapshot** | What the product is, who it is for, and what stage it is at |
| **Current focus** | What is actively being worked on, linked to the documents that say so |
| **Key context docs** | Five to ten important project documents, each with a one-line description |
| **Suggested reading order** | Where a new PM should start, and why |
| **Gaps & follow-up questions for the PM** | What is missing, unclear, or stale |

If your project has a `company-context/` folder from onboarding research, the briefing uses it for the product snapshot rather than listing those documents as reading.

## What it is based on

Only your project. A briefing does not search the web — it synthesises the documents you already have, and links back to each one.

Anything it could not establish from your documents lands in **Gaps & follow-up questions** rather than being filled in with plausible-sounding certainty.

## Where the output goes

The briefing is saved as the output of its background task. Open it from the **Agents** section with **Open output**.

<Tip>
  Generate a briefing before you go on holiday, not after you get back. It is a snapshot of a moment, and the moment worth capturing is the one where the project is still in your head.
</Tip>
